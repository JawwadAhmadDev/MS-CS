# Comparative Analysis of Human vs. LLM Text Detection with Explainability

| Author(s) and Year | Title of the Study | Method Used | Dataset | Explainability Method | Key Findings or Contributions | Limitations (optional) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joshi et al., 2024 | HULLMI: Human vs LLM identification with explainability | Traditional ML (Naive-Bayes, MLP, RF, XGBoost) & Transformers (T5, RoBERTa) | Curated corpora (English Lit, Recipes, Tweets, IMDb, Quora) & OpenGPT/OpenWebText | LIME | Traditional ML performs as well as modern NLP detectors; LIME reveals influential input regions. | Needs dataset expansion beyond ChatGPT; future work on SHAP. |
| Najjar et al., 2025 | Leveraging Explainable AI for LLM Text Attribution: Differentiating Human-Written and Multiple LLM-Generated Text | Random Forest (RF) & Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) | Student-written text vs. 5 LLMs (ChatGPT, LLaMA, Bard, Claude, Perplexity) | XAI Feature Importance | 98.5% accuracy; outperformed GPTZero (78.3%). Enabled source attribution and author profiling. | Focus on student-level writing; may vary with professional texts. |
| Shah et al., 2023 | Detecting and unmasking AI-generated texts through explainable AI using stylistic features | Traditional ML (Stylometric/Lexical features) | Stylistic-feature datasets (word length, syllable count, etc.) | LIME & SHAP | 93% accuracy; Herdan’s C and Simpson’s Index are pivotal features for classification. | Sensitivity to specific stylistic indices may vary across genres. |
| Mitrović et al., 2023 | ChatGPT or Human? Detect and Explain | Fine-tuned Transformer-based Model | Short online reviews (custom queries and rephrased reviews) | SHAP | 79% accuracy; SHAP showed ChatGPT is polite, impersonal, and uses fancy/atypical vocabulary. | Detection harder on rephrased text; restricted to short reviews. |
| Hadi et al., 2023 | Towards Explainable AI-Generated Text Detection Using Ensemble | Ensemble & Mixed-model strategy | English and Dutch texts across multiple genres | SHAP | Ensemble approach improves robustness; SHAP provides clearer insights into model decisions. | Complexity of ensemble models may increase computational overhead. |
| Ardeshirifar, 2025 | Comparing hand-crafted and deep learning approaches | Hand-crafted features vs. RoBERTa | Human ChatGPT Comparison Corpus (HC3) & GPT-2 outputs | Feature Analysis | RoBERTa (98% F1) significantly outperformed hand-crafted methods (94% F1) in generalization. | Hand-crafted features struggled with cross-dataset generalization. |
| Grabska-Gradzińska et al., 2025 | Stylometry recognizes human and LLM-generated texts in short samples | Tree-based models (Decision Trees, LightGBM) with Stylometry | Wikipedia summaries vs. GPT-3.5/4, LLaMa 2/3, Orca, Falcon | SHAP | Up to 0.98 accuracy on GPT-4; LLMs show greater grammatical standardization than humans. | Limited to well-defined text types (e.g., encyclopedic). |
| McGovern et al., 2024 | Your Large Language Models Are Leaving Fingerprints | Simple Classifiers on N-gram & POS features | Five datasets analyzing lexical and morphosyntactic features | Fingerprint Visualization | LLMs possess unique morphosyntactic fingerprints; chat-tuned models are easier to detect. | Fingerprints may change as training data and model versions evolve. |
| Adilazuarda et al., 2023 | Beyond Turing: A Comparative Analysis | Traditional ML, LM Fine-tuning, & Multilingual Fine-tuning | Broad range of machine-generated texts | Comparative Performance Analysis | Significant differences in performance across methods; transformer fine-tuning excels. | Focus on binary classification; lacks specific post-hoc XAI tools like LIME. |
| Masih et al., 2025 | Classifying human vs. AI text with ML and explainable transformers | Machine Learning & Transformer Models | General AI/Human Text Corpora | LIME & SHAP | Integration of XAI tools is necessary for production-level reliability in education/media. | Performance metrics vary based on text domain. |
| Mc, 2025 | Classifying AI vs. Human Content: Integrating BERT and Linguistic Features | Hybrid: BERT + Engineered Linguistic Features | General text classification datasets | LIME | Hybrid models provide better interpretability by highlighting specific linguistic drivers. | Feature engineering is time-consuming compared to pure deep learning. |
| Ali & Yousif, 2024 | Interpretable Multi-Label Classification of Human-and Large Language Model-Generated Texts | Transformer Embeddings & XGBoost | Multi-label text source attribution dataset | SHAP & LIME | Accurately identifies specific LLM sources; XAI explains multi-label decisions. | High resource requirement for embedding large volumes of text. |
| Baharifar et al., 2025 | Interpretable AI for Classifying Medical Misinformation | Multi-Modal Feature Models | Human-written vs. LLM-generated medical misinformation | SHAP & LIME | LLM-generated misinformation is often more neutral; XAI identifies deceptive patterns. | Specific to medical domain; requires expert validation of misinformation labels. |
| Mohammadi et al., 2025 | Explainability-based token replacement on llm-generated text | Ensemble Classifier with Token Replacement | Multi-language and multi-domain AIGT corpora | SHAP & LIME | XAI tools can identify tokens for replacement to hide AI origins; ensembles remain robust. | Token replacement can degrade the semantic quality of the generated text. |
| Iqbal & Kashem, 2025 | A Machine Learning Framework for Identifying Sources of AI-Generated Text | Supervised ML Framework | Multi-LLM output dataset (GPT, Llama, etc.) | Feature Importance Ranking | Successfully attributes text to specific model families with high precision. | Generalization to newer versions of LLMs remains a challenge. |
| Anonymous, 2025 | Assessing Classical ML and Transformer-based Approaches for Research Text | DistilBERT, BERT variants, & Logistic Regression | 250 pairs of research abstracts | Model Comparison Analysis | DistilBERT achieved the best performance; ensembles did not outperform single transformers. | Small dataset size (250 pairs); limited generalizability to non-research text. |
| Breneur et al., 2025 | NOTAI. AI: Explainable Detection via Curvature and Feature Attribution | Geometric (Curvature) analysis & Feature Attribution | Adversarially modified LLM text | Feature Attribution Mapping | Curvature metrics reveal intrinsic LLM signatures invisible to standard NLP models. | High mathematical complexity; harder to implement for real-time detection. |
